# Rule-kind census for the v24 decision

Reviewed code: v23 `70ce3b4`, compared with v22 `e870198`. Sources: https://www.freyavoice.ai/hackathon (fetched during this review), briefs/brief-a.md and brief-b.md, hidden/dry-1/brief.md, hidden/dry-2/{01-mail.txt,02-policy.md,03-memo.txt,04-codes.txt}, policy.py, and the consumers in compile.py/workflow_runtime.py/bankcal.py. I did not author the schema. Likelihood is a relative judgment from the published scope, not information about the hidden briefs.

The organizer promises variations in identity questions, limits/deadlines, outcomes and calendars, plus clean/raw document forms. It does not promise that every real-world collections practice will appear. The same seven operations remain, so this list prioritizes rules implementable through those operations. A hidden case needs Outcome + Policy + Honesty on all three repetitions; fluent prompt-only approximations do not protect the ledger.

**Meaning of “today”:** assume the extractor follows its guide, records an unrepresentable rule in `unresolved` and otherwise emits a valid nearest/default structural policy. In that path v23 **ignores** the unresolved rule: `compile.embed_policy` empties it and `default_prompts` no longer includes it (compile.py:83,210). Neither `policy.validate` nor build's compile gate rejects a nonempty unresolved list. If instead the extractor invents a typed field, unsupported kind or forbidden false flag, construction/validation **fails the build** after repair/fallback attempts; that is not the phone agent crashing. Runtime exceptions are generally caught by `_run` and routed toward a technical-error close. This distinction matters more than the label “unsupported.”

| Rank | Likelihood | Plausible unexpressible rule kind | What the agent would do today |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | High | Composite deadline: earlier of N business days and month end, an absolute campaign cap, or next contractual due date | **Default/ignore:** choose one primitive deadline and ignore the other cap; no min/max/reference operator exists in DeadlineRule (policy.py:21–28). |
| 2 | High | Deadline/band conditioned on product type, balance or a returned risk flag, not just days late | **Default:** `_band` selects only a global days-late interval, so equal-lateness products share the deadline even when policy requires different rules (policy.py:115; workflow_runtime.py:182). |
| 3 | High | Derived floor: percentage, greater of two amounts, rounding rule, or cap at full arrears | **Default:** `_floor_of` reads one response property (or full amount), never computes a policy expression; an unresolved formula is ignored (policy.py:125; runtime:177). |
| 4 | High | Per-question wrong-answer budgets or an immediate lockout on one security field | **Default:** only the global limit/API remaining counter controls exhaustion, so a forbidden retry remains possible (policy.py:52; runtime:534–544). |
| 5 | High–medium | Weekday-dependent callback intervals, lunch gaps or several disjoint windows | **Default:** one start/end interval applies to all allowed callback dates; validation rejects a richer interval structure if forced into the current fields (policy.py:69; bankcal.py:269). |
| 6 | Medium–high | Callback within N business days, holder-requested callback after verification, or days excluded independently of promise calendar | **Default/ignore:** day selection is limited to today/next-business-day/both and scheduling is wired to the third-party stage; unsupported day enums fail validation rather than gaining semantics. |
| 7 | Medium–high | Conditional identity: any two of three questions, alternate check after a failed field, or a format-specific normalizer/checksum | **Default:** every listed question is asked sequentially with date/digits/year/text normalization; there is no branch, alternatives or policy-defined text transformation (policy.py:38,52; compile.py:319). |
| 8 | Medium | Minimum lead time or same-day payment cutoff, e.g. after 16:00 earliest promise is next business day | **Default:** any business-day today remains valid regardless of time; `promise_today_allowed=false` is explicitly compile-rejected, and a cutoff is not expressible. |
| 9 | Medium | Product prioritization other than API order, or a total-call persuasion budget shared across products | **Default:** runtime traverses API order and per-product counters; there is no priority comparator or global attempt budget (policy.py:187; runtime:670). |
| 10 | Medium | Conditional outcome priority, e.g. unresolved dispute overrides a promise, or note reason ranked by severity instead of first non-paid product | **Default:** finalization uses fixed transfer/promise/all-paid/first-non-paid precedence; outcome names can change but a new precedence expression cannot (policy.py:158; runtime:st_finalize). |
| 11 | Medium | New special intent such as complaint, insolvency, represented-by-solicitor or cease-contact with its own action | **Ignore:** SpecialCase can store arbitrary names/triggers/actions, but the fixed extractor enums and `_special` dispatch do not route new names; storage is not operational support (compile.py:252; runtime:782). |
| 12 | Medium | State-specific mandatory disclosure/consent, e.g. explicit consent before transfer, or a different sentence when floor accepted versus full | **Ignore/default:** Phrases has fixed insertion points and there is no generic guarded statement/action sequence or required-consent state for new rules. |
| 13 | Medium–low | Recurring/observed holidays, partial business days, region/product calendars, or timezone conversion | **Default:** a single weekday set plus finite ISO holiday list is used; no calendar selection, recurrence, cutoff or timezone parameter exists (policy.py:97). |
| 14 | Medium–low | Two dated instalment promises on one product, or a required per-product confirmation instead of a batch | **Build failure if represented literally**, because one_promise_per_product and one_confirmation_for_batch must be true; if left unresolved, the current one-write/batch policy wins. |
| 15 | Lower | Retry schedule conditioned on HTTP status or read/transfer/note failures, backoff specified by policy | **Default:** PromiseWritePolicy holds only an extra promise-attempt count; other operation failure handling/backoff is fixed, not policy-driven. |

## Existing fields that are not new schema kinds

Do not spend v24 adding values already representable: arbitrary ordered identity fields, digit lengths, MDY/DMY, global wrong limits, explicit holidays/custom weekdays, the four primitive deadline kinds, type-to-floor-field mappings, bounded product count, note enablement, renamed outcome/reason/queue codes, promise retry count, human ask-once and hardship end/continue/takes-promise. Question order and signed memo precedence are extraction correctness problems, not missing dataclasses.

Conversely, some existing switches are not honored consistently by their consumers: `_calendar_dict()` forces `missing_date_parts='next_occurrence'` despite the Calendar field; one common business-day calendar applies everywhere; several outcome/date/amount booleans have fixed template behavior. `SpecialCase.action` being an arbitrary dictionary does not make arbitrary control flow supported. These need consumer audits, not additional nearly-identical schema fields.

The dry-2 memo already exposes an important boundary: “last business day of current month; do not roll into next month.” v23 instead rewrites an expired deadline to next business day (runtime:581–582). That is a correctness defect in an existing kind, not justification for silently broadening the policy.

## Recommended v24 scope

First fix the proven v23 regressions in review.md. Then make unsupported-rule handling explicit in the build result and action gates; a nonempty unresolved list must not disappear while the run looks fully correct. Use a conservative, declared per-action fallback only where the brief defines one; do not invent a promise cap or outcome.

For extension work, the highest-value compact addition is a small typed expression system for deadlines and floors with explicit rounding and no-eligible-date behavior. Next add per-field identity limits and a list of callback weekday/window predicates. These address ranks 1/3/4/5 with direct offline acceptance fixtures. Add product predicates if time remains. Avoid replacing typed arithmetic with free-form executable policy prose.

New packages: tests/fixtures/external/codex-cedar-limits (composite deadline + calculated floor), and codex-harbor-exceptions (per-field lockout + disjoint weekday callback windows). Each lists exactly two deliberate gaps and supplies supported-field and behavioral oracles. No hidden organizer input was used.
